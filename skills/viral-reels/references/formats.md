# Reel formats

A format is a reusable skeleton. Pick the one that fits the idea and what the user can film, then fill it with the chosen chain. Timings are guides; cut anything that does not earn its place.

## Formats for showing or selling something

### 1. Ad disguised as a scene (20-40 s)

Use when: there is a product and the goal is sales interest without the "ad" reflex.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | A recognisable situation with a small tension (stop: story or familiarity) |
| 3-20 | The situation builds so the viewer predicts where it goes (hold: prediction) |
| 20-30 | The twist, and the product is the reason for it (pay off: product as punchline) |
| last 2 | Brand or product name, nothing more |

### 2. Tension to reveal (10-25 s)

Use when: a physical product can be hidden, frozen, wrapped, covered or otherwise put "under threat".

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-2 | The object and the thing about to happen to it |
| 2-12 | The build, slowed down just before the moment |
| 12-18 | The reveal, clean and unhurried |
| 18-22 | The product in use or on a person |

### 3. Fact with a twist (25-45 s)

Use when: the user is an expert or sells something whose value can be attached to a surprising truth.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | A question most people answer wrongly, shown with a striking image |
| 3-25 | Why the common answer is wrong, with visuals that change every few seconds |
| 25-35 | The better option, which turns out to be the offer |
| last 3 | One call to action |

### 4. Talk with busy hands (20-45 s)

Use when: the user can only film themselves and the content is advice or opinion.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | Hands start a task; first line is the claim, not a greeting |
| 3-35 | The talk, one idea, while the task progresses |
| end | The task finishes on the final point |

### 5. Frame break (6-15 s, loops)

Use when: brand awareness and rewatches; there is editing skill available.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | The video looks like an ordinary post with interface and caption |
| 3-10 | The subject crosses or spills over the fake interface |
| end | A caption joke that matches the stunt; cut so it loops |

### 6. Behind the scenes (20-40 s)

Use when: growing follows; the work has a visible process.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | The setup or an attempt in progress, numbered if there are several |
| 3-30 | The messy process, including what went wrong |
| end | Stop before the finished result and point to where it is |

### 7. Witness (15-30 s)

Use when: reach and shares; a second person can film.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-3 | A stranger seen from a distance, with a judging caption |
| 3-15 | Something shifts: the person does the unexpected, or the filming turns deliberate |
| end | The flip that vindicates them |

### 8. Process interrupted (15-30 s)

Use when: craft, food, making; the user has a calm process to show.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-4 | The calm, satisfying process |
| 4-8 | Something goes wrong |
| 8-25 | What happens next, held open to the end |

## Formats for teaching or commentary

### 9. Outlier breakdown (35-50 s)

Use when: the user teaches marketing, a craft or a skill and can analyse someone else's success.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-10 | The example itself, raw, with no commentary |
| 10-15 | Proof of the result and one surprising fact about it |
| 15-40 | Two or three named reasons, each one line plus a replay of the moment |
| 40-45 | "That's the formula" and a three-word recap |
| last 5 | Call to action |

### 10. Numbered method (30-50 s)

Open on the result the method produces, then three steps at most, each shown being done. End on the one rule that makes it work.

### 11. Example montage (15-30 s)

Four to six quick examples of one technique with almost no words. Works as a "save this" reference. Name the technique on screen once.

### 12. Fill-in-the-blank templates (25-35 s)

Three reusable patterns, each shown as a blank pattern and then as a real example. The viewer should be able to use one immediately.

### 13. "N mistakes" list (40-55 s)

Open on the worst mistake as an image. One line of principle per mistake, with a wrong-then-right visual pair.

### 14. Flop versus hit (30-50 s)

Two versions of the same thing with very different results. Name the two or three changes that made the difference. Strong because the comparison controls for everything else.

### 15. Same format, different creators (30-45 s)

Show two or three creators succeeding with one structure, then name the structure. Proves the format, not the person, did the work.

## Series

Whatever the format, a repeated opening turns single reels into a series: same first shot, same framing, new subject. Recognition does part of the stop beat's work from the second episode on, and it gives followers a reason to come back.
