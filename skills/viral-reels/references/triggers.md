## How to use this file

Each trigger lists the **mechanism** (why a brain responds), ways to **stage it**, and how it **fails**. The mechanism is the part to hold on to: a trigger staged without its mechanism is only a gimmick. When you propose one, always translate it into a concrete image for this user's niche and name the question it puts in the viewer's head.

The triggers in different beats must connect. The stop trigger opens a question, the hold trigger keeps it open, the pay-off trigger answers it (or refuses to, on purpose).

## Beat 1: stop the scroll

The job: earn the first one to three seconds. The opening image matters more than the opening words.

### Danger or urgency

- **Mechanism**: possible harm gets priority processing. The viewer is watching before deciding to watch.
- **Stage it**: an action about to go wrong, something balanced badly, a near-miss, a clock running out. For experts, open inside the costly mistake as it happens instead of announcing a tip about it.
- **Question raised**: "is something bad about to happen?"
- **Fails when**: nothing is truly at stake, or it relies on a fake warning about safety or health. Film the look of risk, never real risk.

### Pattern interrupt

- **Mechanism**: the feed runs on autopilot. Anything that breaks what the brain predicts for this kind of video forces a second look.
- **Stage it**: a familiar object in the wrong place or at the wrong size; a calm process that suddenly goes wrong; the normal activity in an abnormal location; one detail that should not be there.
- **Question raised**: "wait, what is that?"
- **Fails when**: the oddity has nothing to do with the idea (the viewer stops, then feels tricked), or it is not visible in the very first frame.

### Broken reality (frame break)

- **Mechanism**: viewers assume a video is a flat rectangle that behaves like every other post. When content seems to spill over the interface, or something appears to sit on their own screen, there is a split second of "is that real?". It stops the thumb and invites a rewatch.
- **Stage it**: dress the video as an ordinary post and let the subject cross the fake interface; put something "on the lens"; let liquid or an object leave its frame. Often needs no words.
- **Question raised**: "is that actually on my screen?"
- **Fails when**: the fake interface is sloppy, or it imitates a system alert in a way that alarms people. Needs some editing skill.

### Witness hook

- **Mechanism**: the viewer is placed as a bystander watching a stranger, with a caption that passes judgment. People take a side at once (sympathy, amusement, disapproval) and any of them holds attention.
- **Stage it**: filmed from a distance, observational, with a caption stating a judgment the video will overturn.
- **Question raised**: "what is going on with this person?"
- **Fails when**: the subject really is being mocked. The ending has to vindicate them.

### Familiarity

- **Mechanism**: ordinary people in an ordinary place read as "that could be me", so the video feels personal instead of produced. It also lowers the guard, so the later surprise lands harder.
- **Stage it**: real staff, real location, phone camera, a situation the audience has lived through.
- **Question raised**: "I know this situation; where is it going?"
- **Fails when**: nothing then breaks the normality. Familiar with no twist is just dull.

### Movement hook

- **Mechanism**: eyes track motion involuntarily. Hands doing a task while someone talks keep the eyes busy before the topic has earned interest.
- **Stage it**: cutting, packing, setting up, drawing, assembling; a task with visible progress and its own small finish.
- **Question raised**: "what are they making, and what are they about to say?"
- **Fails when**: the movement competes with the message. Enough to keep watching, not enough to distract.

### Borrowed hook (stitch)

- **Mechanism**: open with two or three seconds of a clip that has already proved it stops people, then cut to yourself reacting or continuing. The attention is earned by the clip and spent on you.
- **Stage it**: use the platform's stitch or remix feature so the original creator is credited; make your first line connect directly to what was just shown.
- **Question raised**: whatever the borrowed clip raised, plus "what does this person have to say about it?"
- **Fails when**: the cut to you is a non sequitur, or the clip is taken without credit.

### Curiosity hook

- **Mechanism**: an open question the viewer cannot answer creates an itch. The strongest forms are a question whose obvious answer you declare wrong, a result reached by an unexpected method, a message that is hidden or unreadable, and a forced choice between two options.
- **Stage it**: the workhorse for experts and educators. Show the thing while stating the claim.
- **Question raised**: "why is my answer wrong?" or "which one is it?"
- **Fails when**: the answer is obvious, is given immediately, or the claim is not true.

### Is this real?

- **Mechanism**: a staged moment filmed as if caught by accident makes the viewer try to work out whether it is real. That evaluation is watch time.
- **Stage it**: handheld, imperfect framing, an "I happened to be recording" feel, resolved as a bit by the end.
- **Question raised**: "did that actually happen?"
- **Fails when**: it deceives about real harm or real facts, or the playful resolution never comes.

### Behind the scenes

- **Mechanism**: the messy making-of feels like exclusive access, and access builds trust.
- **Stage it**: the setup, failed takes, numbered attempts, the crew, the workshop.
- **Question raised**: "what are they making, and will it work?"
- **Fails when**: the process is not visually interesting, or no result is promised.

## Beat 2: hold attention

The job: keep the question open through the middle. This is where most reels lose people.

### Prediction trap

- **Mechanism**: once a brain predicts an outcome it needs to check whether it was right.
- **Stage it**: make the trajectory obvious, then delay the outcome.
- **Fails when**: the outcome is exactly what was predicted, or the setup is too confusing to predict anything.

### Open loop

- **Mechanism**: an unanswered question feels incomplete, and people stay for closure.
- **Stage it**: start an action and withhold its result; "what is inside?", "will it fit?", "is he going to do it?". Keep the unresolved thing on screen.
- **Fails when**: the loop closes too early, or the video wanders off and the viewer forgets it was open.

### Tension before the payoff

- **Mechanism**: stretching the moment before a reveal makes the reveal hit harder.
- **Stage it**: a wind-up, a pause, a countdown, one extra beat before the result.
- **Fails when**: the pause is dead air. Something must still be building.

### Escalation

- **Mechanism**: each step is harder than the last, so viewers stay for the final level.
- **Stage it**: easy, hard, impossible; small, medium, absurd. Label the levels in one word each.
- **Fails when**: the last level is not clearly bigger than the first.

### Dual attention

- **Mechanism**: ears follow the words while eyes follow an action, so attention has nowhere to drift.
- **Stage it**: pair any stretch of talking with a visible process.
- **Fails when**: the two tracks contradict or overload each other.

### Shared waiting

- **Mechanism**: people on screen are waiting or confused too, and the viewer waits with them.
- **Stage it**: reaction shots of others trying to work it out.
- **Fails when**: the reactions look acted.

### Delayed reveal

- **Mechanism**: holding the product or the answer back until late means retention carries all the way to it.
- **Stage it**: the product is absent or unrecognisable until the pay-off beat.
- **Fails when**: the wait is not rewarded and the viewer feels sold to.

### Story

- **Mechanism**: a person who wants something and meets an obstacle makes viewers ask what happens next, even in ten seconds.
- **Stage it**: one character, one want, one complication.
- **Fails when**: there is setup with no complication.

## Beat 3: pay off

The job: answer the question in a way that triggers the behaviour the reel is for.

| Trigger | Mechanism | Tends to produce |
|---|---|---|
| **Unexpected twist** | The outcome contradicts the prediction, ideally changing what the opening meant | Rewatches, shares |
| **Emotional misdirection** | The setup builds worry or pity; the reveal flips to relief or laughter | Replays ("how was I fooled?") and shares |
| **Satisfying reveal** | A clean visual answer with no pitch: the result, the fit, the transformation | Completion, saves |
| **Product as punchline** | The product appears at the exact moment of surprise and is the reason for it, so the ad reads as the joke's answer | Sales interest without the "ad" reflex |
| **Missing payoff** | The video stops before the result and points to the profile or next part | Profile visits, follows |
| **Unclosed loop** | The main line resolves but one thread is left dangling | Comments asking about it |
| **Human imperfection** | A mistake is left in, or is the point | Connection, relatability |
| **Emotional payoff** | A story ends in warmth, pride or relief | Shares to specific people |

Notes on using them:

- A twist should make the opening worth watching again. If knowing the ending changes nothing about the start, it is only a surprise.
- Missing payoff and unclosed loop withhold on purpose. Use them only when the withheld thing really exists where you point to, and not on every reel.
- Product as punchline is the difference between an ad people skip and an ad people share: do not interrupt the content with the product, make the product the answer.

## Multipliers

These are not beats. They raise the ceiling of any chain.

- **Timing**: tie the hook to a season, event or moment the audience is already thinking about.
- **Word economy**: cut on-screen text to labels a viewer could follow without the language. Friction limits reach.
- **Imperfection**: a polished take is predictable; a real stumble is not.
- **Sound-off legibility**: the hook and payoff should work muted.
- **Repetition of winners**: when an opening works, reuse it with a new subject. Series beat one-offs.
- **Structure, not content**: a format that travelled once can travel again with a fresh twist. Take the psychology and build new content.

## Chains that have worked

Use these as starting points, not scripts. Each comes from a real outlier (see `case-library.md`).

| Stop | Hold | Pay off | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Danger | Prediction trap | Impossible twist | Pure reach |
| Danger (warning) | Open loop ("what's inside?") | Surprise reveal, with timing | Seasonal promotion |
| Pattern interrupt | Open loop | Satisfying reveal | Product launch with no pitch |
| Pattern interrupt | Open loop | Attention transfer to product | Brand ad |
| Story | Prediction | Product as punchline | Brand ad as entertainment |
| Familiarity | Prediction trap | Ad as punchline | Local or service business |
| Witness | Pattern break | Flipped expectation | Reach and shares |
| Curiosity (your answer is wrong) | Explanation with visuals | The better option is the offer | Experts, real estate, education |
| Danger or object under threat | Tension, slowed | Satisfying reveal | Physical products |
| Movement | Dual attention | The talk's own point | Talking-head education |
| Behind the scenes | Process | Missing payoff | Growing follows |
| Familiarity (normal-looking post) | none needed, 6-15 s | Frame break | Rewatches, brand awareness |
| Calm process | Something goes wrong | Human imperfection | Craft, food, hospitality |
| Sad or tense setup | Prediction | Emotional misdirection | Shares |

## Choosing by what can be filmed

| The user can film | Strong stop triggers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Only themselves talking | Curiosity, movement, borrowed hook | Add a hands task; never open on a greeting |
| A physical product | Pattern interrupt (scale or place), object under threat, frame break | Hold the product's use for the payoff |
| A place and staff (local or service business) | Familiarity, behind the scenes, witness, story | Real people beat actors |
| A screen (software, games, charts, boards) | Curiosity, broken reality, flop versus hit comparison | Zoom to one detail; the full screen is too busy |
| Nothing (text, stock, animation) | Curiosity plus a numbered format, or a breakdown of someone else's outlier | Word-by-word text on a plain background works |
