It worked because each of its three beats does one job: a threat stops you, a guess holds you, and a joke pays you off. That structure transfers to a dental clinic; the croissants don't.

I'm working from your description, not the video, so I can't judge its sound, exact timing or whether the knock was staged.

## 1. What happens

A tray of flawless croissants gets knocked toward the counter edge by the baker's own elbow. The video freezes just before the fall and asks you to guess the survivors, then makes you sit through a one-by-one count. It ends on the baker eating the only casualty, relabelled "quality control".

## 2. The chain: threat, guess, bite

- **Stop: danger (an object under threat).** The first frame shows something perfect that clearly took work, and a second later it is about to be destroyed. Possible loss gets your attention before you decide to give it. The calm start matters: the perfect tray is the expectation the elbow breaks. Question raised: "are they all going to hit the floor?"
- **Hold: prediction trap, made explicit.** Most reels let you predict silently. This one freezes at the peak and tells you to commit to a number, and once you have committed you need to check it. The count then pays the answer out one croissant at a time, so there is no moment where you can leave with the answer. Question kept open: "was I right?"
- **Pay off: emotional misdirection, landed with human imperfection.** You were braced for a ruined batch at a tiny bakery. You get relief (nearly all survived), then a laugh (she eats the evidence). The hook's question is answered exactly, with a number, and the mistake becomes the charm. Nothing is pitched, yet the last image is someone enjoying the product.

## 3. Multipliers

- **Word economy.** Six words of text in the whole reel. The fall and the count need no language at all.
- **Sound-off.** From your description, nothing depends on audio.
- **Imperfection.** The accident is the content, not a flaw in it.
- **A built-in comment.** "Guess how many" gives people the easiest thing there is to comment: a number.
- **A proven skeleton.** Calm craft, something goes wrong, outcome held to the end. The same shape shows up in other outliers I have on file: a potter's calm video where the pots collapse, and a coffee pour whose failed take far out-viewed the flawless one.

One mechanism here is not in the trigger library this method uses: the **counted reveal**, where the answer arrives one unit at a time and every count updates the viewer's guess. It is worth adding to `references/triggers.md` under the hold beat.

## 4. Outlier check

2.1 million views on about 3,000 followers is roughly 700 times the audience. Even if every follower watched, they are under 0.2% of the views, so this travelled almost entirely to strangers. What I can't see is the bakery's usual views, or whether they have reused this opening since. Check their grid: if a second reel with the same opening also beat their normal numbers, the format did the work and not luck.

## 5. What to take

1. Open on something finished and perfect, so its value is obvious in one frame.
2. Put it at risk within two seconds, by accident, and stop before the outcome.
3. Ask for a guess with a number in it, in about five words.
4. Reveal the answer by counting, not by showing the result at once.
5. Make the outcome better than feared, and turn the one loss into a joke on the maker, never on the product or the customer.
6. Sell nothing.

One thing does not transfer. A clumsy baker loses a croissant; a clumsy clinic loses trust. So the object under threat has to be harmless and non-clinical, and the count is where you look careful.

## 6. A version for your clinic

Assumptions, since I can't ask: the goal is reach and local recognition, not direct bookings; you can film in the clinic with a phone and two willing team members; and you have a demonstration jaw model with at least one tooth that lifts out. No reel is guaranteed views. This one removes the avoidable reasons a reel fails.

### The dropped jaw

**Idea in one sentence:** A demonstration jaw gets knocked off the front desk and is given a completely serious check-up to count the survivors.
**Goal:** reach
**Format:** Process interrupted
**Length:** 23 s
**Chain:** Stop: danger (object under threat) -> Hold: prediction trap with a counted reveal -> Pay off: emotional misdirection + human imperfection ("threat, guess, extraction")

#### Hook (0-3 s)

- **See:** a spotless full set of model teeth at the very edge of the reception counter; an elbow clips it and it tips over.
- **Read:** guess how many teeth survived
- **Hear:** ordinary reception noise that cuts dead on the freeze. No voice.
- **Question it opens:** "how many teeth are about to come out?"

**Alternates to test**

1. Put the total in the text: "32 teeth. how many made it?" (use your model's real number), so guessing is easier.
2. Calm start: gloved hands set the polished jaw down with visible pride, one beat of stillness, then the knock. Slower, but the loss feels bigger.
3. Caught by accident: the phone is filming something else, such as a team member waving at the camera, and the knock happens in the background.

#### Beat sheet

| Time | On screen | Text overlay | Voice / sound | Trigger at work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0-2 s | Close on the jaw at the counter edge. A team member turns with an armful of files; her elbow clips it and it tips. | none | Reception noise | Stop: danger |
| 2-4 s | Freeze on the jaw mid-tip. | guess how many teeth survived | Sound cuts out | Hold: prediction trap |
| 4-7 s | The jaw is lying on the dental chair headrest wearing a patient bib. The dentist swings the light over, entirely serious. | none | Light clicks on | Hold: open loop, with a second surprise |

| 19-23 s | The dentist holds the missing tooth up to camera in tweezers, deadpan. | routine extraction | none | Pay off: emotional misdirection + human imperfection |
| Caption | Not on screen | none | none | Call to action |

#### Payoff and call to action

- **Payoff:** the hook asks how many survived, and the counter answers with the real number, one short. The accident is then renamed as a procedure.
- **Call to action:** comment your guess, asked in the caption only.

#### Caption and cover

- **Caption:** Comment your guess before the count finishes. The patient is resting comfortably.
- **Hashtags:** #dentist #dentalhumor #[yourcity]dentist
- **Cover text:** how many survived?

#### Shoot and edit notes

- **Needed:** a phone, two team members, the jaw model, a bib, the chair, a mouth mirror. No patients and no other instruments in shot.
- **The one shot that must be right:** the knock. Teeth clearly visible in frame one, elbow in by second one, a single take that looks accidental. Prop the phone on a shelf so it feels caught, not filmed. Someone catches the jaw below frame; the fall is never shown, so nothing has to break.
- **Where to slow down, what to cut:** slow the last five teeth and hold the pause at the gap. Cut any intro, logo, or shot of picking the jaw up. Film the knock at the front desk, never in the treatment room.

#### Check

- **Passes:** stop, hold, pay off, craft, honesty and safety. The count on screen must be the model's real count, and the bib scene makes clear it is a bit.
- **Risks left:** a plastic jaw matters less to a viewer than a morning's croissants, so the opening depends on the teeth looking pristine and the knock looking real. The count is also longer than the bakery's; if the sped-up middle drags, people leave before the punchline.
- **Test first:** alternate 3, if viewers drop in the first three seconds.

If you would rather this drove bookings than reach, keep everything and change only the last shot so that a service you really offer is the punchline, for example the tooth clicking back into place.
