## Three ways to open it

The first seconds decide most of the result, so here are three versions that differ in how they stop the scroll.

**A. "Interrupt, loop, punchline"** (recommended)

- **Stop, pattern interrupt:** a real board where one knight is wearing a bright sticky note that says FREE, and a hand takes it. The viewer thinks: why does that piece say FREE?
- **Hold, open loop:** the tag vanishes ("what you see"), then a chess.com position with no tags and a 3-2-1 countdown to find the free piece. The viewer thinks: which one is it, and how do I learn to see that?
- **Pay off, product as punchline:** you pick up the FREE tag and stick it on your laptop with Zoom open. The only thing that should be free is the first lesson.

**B. "Danger, prediction, rescue"**

- **Stop, danger:** a hand holding the queen, hovering over a pawn that is clearly bait, with the text "Don't take the pawn". The viewer thinks: what happens if they take it?
- **Hold, prediction trap:** the hand goes for it anyway, slowly. The viewer expects a disaster and stays to see if they're right.
- **Pay off, product as punchline:** a second hand comes into frame and stops the first. That hand is you, and that is what a lesson is.

**C. "Curiosity, explanation, offer"**

- **Stop, curiosity:** a chess.com game review zoomed in on a red "??" blunder mark, with the text "Adult beginners: stop studying openings". The viewer thinks: why is that wrong, and what should I do instead?
- **Hold, dual attention:** you explain while three quick clips show the same kind of blunder in three different games.
- **Pay off, the better option is the offer:** what fixes it is someone going through your own games with you, which is the trial lesson. Only use this one if you actually believe the openings claim.

Go with A. A lot of chess content is a screen-recorded board, so a sticky note on a real piece doesn't look like the rest of the feed. It also works with the sound off, and the tag people have been staring at ends up on the offer, so the pitch is the punchline instead of an interruption.

# The FREE tag

**Idea in one sentence:** Your opponent can see which of your pieces are free to take and you can't yet, and learning to see it is what the free first lesson is for.  
**Goal:** sales (leads: free trial bookings)  
**Format:** Fact with a twist, shot at the board  
**Length:** 30 s  
**Chain:** Stop: pattern interrupt -> Hold: open loop -> Pay off: product as punchline ("interrupt, loop, punchline")

## Hook (0-3 s)

- **See:** a close shot of a real board mid-game. One knight wears a bright sticky-note flag that says FREE, big enough to read instantly. Within the first second a hand comes in with a bishop and takes it.
- **Read:** What your opponent sees
- **Hear:** the clack of the capture, then "This is what your opponent sees."
- **Question it opens:** why does that piece say FREE, and why can my opponent see that when I can't?

**Alternates to test**

1. **Screen first.** Open on the chess.com recording instead of the real board, with a FREE label added in CapCut sitting on the undefended piece, and the opponent taking it. Same text, same line. This tells you whether the real board or the screen stops your audience better.
2. **Straight to the lens.** You hold the captured knight, tag still on it, right up to the camera. Read: "You gave me this for free". Hear: the same line. Then cut to the board.
3. **Whole board tagged.** Open on a position where five or six pieces all carry FREE tags, your hand pressing on the last one. Read: "Your board, according to your opponent". Hear: the same line.

## Payoff and call to action

- **Payoff:** the hook asks why a piece is tagged FREE and why your opponent sees it when you don't. The answer is that seeing it is a habit somebody has to teach you, and the tag itself moves from the piece to the lesson. FREE stops meaning "you gave this away" and starts meaning "this costs you nothing".
- **Call to action:** book the free trial lesson through the link in bio. Nothing else spoken or on screen, and no "follow for more".

## Caption and cover

- **Caption:** Be honest: did you find it before "one"? If not, that's what the free trial lesson is for. 1-on-1 on Zoom, for adult beginners. Link in bio.
- **Hashtags:** #chessbeginner #adultchess #chesscoach #learnchess
- **Cover text:** What your opponent sees (over the frame of the tagged knight)

## Shoot and edit notes

- **What is needed to film it:** the board, bright sticky notes and a thick marker, your phone propped on books or a tripod, a laptop with a Zoom window open showing only your own camera preview, and one chess.com screen recording. Four shots:
  1. The board position with no tag, held for three seconds. Film this first.
  2. The same position without moving the phone, tag on, bishop takes knight. Cutting from shot 2 back to shot 1 makes the tag disappear.
  3. The chess.com recording. Set a position up in the analysis board, or use a moment from one of your own games, where exactly one piece is attacked and undefended and nothing else is going on. Recording the phone app in portrait saves you cropping. Leave the position still for six seconds, then play the capture. Don't call it a student's game unless it is one and they've agreed.
  4. Your hand picking up the tag and sticking it on the laptop.
- **The one shot that must be right:** the first frame. FREE has to be readable on a phone in the first half second. Cut a strip from the sticky edge of the note, write FREE in thick marker, wrap it round the knight like a flag facing the camera, and get close. If you have to squint at it on your own phone, reshoot closer.
- **Where to slow down, what to cut:** slow down on the tag moving to the laptop, and leave a full beat of silence between "so this tag" and "goes here". Cut any hello, your name, any setting up of pieces and any explanation of what a hanging piece is. In CapCut this is straight cuts plus text: the countdown is three text clips of about a second each, the FREE pop is one text clip with a coloured background, and all text sits in the upper middle of the frame, clear of Instagram's buttons. Skip auto-captions, since the overlays already tell the story with the sound off. Keep the real sound of the captures. If the tag droops or you fumble the piece and it's still readable, keep that take.

## Check

- **Passes:** Stop (the tag is in frame one, there's no greeting, the four-word text works muted). Hold (the question is open from the first second to second 17, there's one idea, the picture changes every three seconds or so, and it slows before the payoff). Pay off (the lesson answers the hook's question and there is one call to action, matched to bookings). Craft (filmable with what you have, and the tag on, tag off, piece taken sequence can be followed without sound). Honesty (no numbers, results or testimonials, and the position is shown as an example). The weakest point is the reason to share, which is not much more than "can you spot it?". That is acceptable for a reel built for bookings.
- **Risks left:** everything depends on the tag being readable in the first frame at phone size. The second risk is the puzzle position: if it's too easy nobody waits for the countdown, and if it's too hard people give up. Show it to someone at your students' level before you film.
- **Test first:** if this underperforms, swap in alternate 2 (the knight held up to the lens, "You gave me this for free") and keep everything after second 3 the same.

Once it's posted, the retention graph tells you what to change. A drop in the first three seconds means the hook, a drop around the countdown means the position, and a drop at the end means the tag move was rushed. If it clearly beats your usual views, make it again with the same opening and a new position before trying a different idea. No reel can promise bookings; this one is built so the avoidable reasons people scroll past are gone.
